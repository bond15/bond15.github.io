0. Background: the two definitions the rest of this note uses

Configurations and the Effect rule. Fix an algebraic theory $\mathcal T = (\Sigma, E)$. Assume its free model monad $T$ is given as a polynomial $p = (S, P)$: a set of shapes $S$, and $P(s) \in \mathbb N$ positions for each shape $s$. This is a monadic container. A configuration is an element $\langle s, M_1, \dots, M_{P(s)}\rangle \in T(\mathrm{Comp}(FA))$: one shape plus one closed computation at each position.

The submission writes the Effect step with a substitution notation, $\langle s, \gamma, M/x_i\rangle$. The rebuttal gave an equivalent derivative form. Here $C$ is a one-hole context of the configuration, $C \in \partial T(\mathrm{Comp}(FA))$: a shape, one distinguished position $i$, and the other $P(s)-1$ subterms. $$C\big[S[\mathsf{op}(\vec M)]\big] \;\mapsto\; \mu\Big(\partial T(\eta)(C)\big[\,\llbracket\mathsf{op}\rrbracket(\eta\,S[M_1],\dots,\eta\,S[M_n])\,\big]\Big)$$ In words:

  1. find an operation at one position, under a stack $S$;
  2. copy the stack into each operand;
  3. interpret the operation in the free model;
  4. wrap every other position in $\eta$;
  5. plug the result back in and flatten with $\mu$.

Affinity (our sense). Fix a shape $s$, a position $i$ and an operation $\mathsf{op}$ of arity $n$. Run the Effect step once on fresh variables: $x_j$ at each $j \neq i$, and $\llbracket\mathsf{op}\rrbracket(\eta y_1,\dots,\eta y_n)$ at $i$. The result has the form $\langle s \circ_i \mathsf{op} ; \rho_{s,i,\mathsf{op}}\rangle$, a new shape plus a renaming $\rho$ of the variables. Call this the generic step. The model is affine iff every $\rho_{s,i,\mathsf{op}}$ is injective, i.e. no Effect step duplicates a subterm. Affine models include writer, list, maybe, errors and finite state. This is not an affine monad in Jacobs' sense.

Naturality lemma. $\mu$, $\eta$ and $\llbracket\mathsf{op}\rrbracket$ are natural transformations. So every Effect step is the generic step with the actual subterms substituted for the variables. This is why the substitution form and the derivative form define the same relation.

2. Required for correctness: bugs we found ourselves

The reviewers did not catch these. We found them while formalizing the paper in Agda and rereading the whole .tex. Each one makes a stated result false or a proof invalid.

  1. The tree-reduction figure (§4) is wrong in three ways.

    • (a) The whole-stack rule $S[\mathsf{op}(\vec M)] \mapsto \mathsf{op}(S[\vec M])$, together with op-congruence as a single step, breaks stack closure. With $M = S[\mathsf{op}(N_1,N_2)]$ and $M \mapsto M'$, both $M\,V$ and $M'\,V$ step to $\mathsf{op}((S\,V)[N_1],(S\,V)[N_2])$, but neither steps to the other. So the following are unjustified:
      • anti-reduction at $\to$ and $\&$ (lines 1622–1632);
      • the congruence lemma;
      • the bind case of the operational fundamental lemma.
    • (b) The side condition S ≠ • on β blocks redexes at the top level. For example, force(thunk(ret ⋆)) is stuck.
    • (c) $\pi_1$-β is missing (only $\pi_2$ is present).

    Together these make the operational fundamental lemma and termination false as stated.

  2. The termination metric is missing 1 + (line 2393). Without it, pure steps need not decrease the metric. The zero case (2439) and Pure case (2446) of the configuration SN proof fail; the Effect case (2451) already silently assumes the 1 +.

  3. Determinism and Progress are asserted, not proved. Line 1723 says "only one reduction applies", with no lemma behind it. The metric, tree SN, anti-reduction and the SN proof all rely on it. We need a Progress-and-determinism lemma in §4.

  4. Two corollaries fail once the theory has equations.

    • The uniqueness corollary for equational canonicity (1459–1477) is stated over unquotiented $\Sigma$-terms. It is false for writer: $\mathsf{tell}_e(\mathsf{ret}\,V)$ and $\mathsf{ret}\,V$ are equal but different terms.
    • Tree canonicity $M \mapsto^* \mathsf{reify}(\llbracket M\rrbracket)$ (1737–1742) is ill-typed. With equations no choice of representative rescues it, since $\mathsf{tell}_e(\mathsf{ret}\,V)$ is already tree-normal.

  5. §5 formula slips.

    • The configuration-soundness calculation applies $\llbracket\mathsf{op}\rrbracket$ to the wrong argument (line 2249). The proof also needs algebraicity of stacks, which it does not mention.
    • The definition of operational model is garbled: "generated by $T$" should be "generated by $X$".

Two ways to repair 1(a):

  • Option A, frame rules. Replace the whole-stack rule with one rule per stack frame, each moving an operation out one frame: $\mathsf{op}(\vec M)\ \mathsf{to}\ x.N \mapsto \mathsf{op}(\vec{M_i\ \mathsf{to}\ x.N})$ and $\mathsf{op}(\vec M)\,V \mapsto \mathsf{op}(\vec{M_i\,V})$. Allow head steps under any stack, including $\bullet$, and keep congruence only in $\mapsto^*$.
    • Reduction becomes deterministic and closed under stacks.
    • Anti-reduction becomes a three-line proof, and tree SN becomes immediate.
    • This matches the RMRCanonicity Agda development.
  • Option B, keep the paper's rule. Restrict anti-reduction to head steps, and prove it together with forward preservation using determinism of head steps. This is what the Cubical Agda port of the paper does (branch popl-formalization).

Neither repaired proof has been checked in full on paper.

4. Required: position the Effect rule against prior work

The key fact. Configurations living in an effect monad and stepped through $\mu$ is not new. Three works already have effect steps of our shape: Gavazzo–Faggian 2021, Gavazzo–Treglia–Vanoni 2024 and Dagnino–Giannini–Zucca 2025. Abou-Saleh–Pattinson 2013 has free-model transition systems. None is cited in the submission. Our "$Q = T$ is new" framing from earlier notes is false.

What we can claim (closest work first):

  1. an Effect rule that fires one operation at one position, chosen by a one-hole context of the polynomial, flattened by $\mu$;
  2. that every step preserves an independently defined denotational semantics;
  3. strong normalization whenever no effect step duplicates (affinity), with the final configuration equal to the denotation at ground types;
  4. one generic theory covering global state (outside GF21), errors and weighted lists (outside plain comodels), for typed CBPV.

Do not claim:

  • that configurations in $T$ are new;
  • "errors have no runner"; say "no ordinary comodel";
  • that we generalize comodels;
  • that $\lambda_{coop}$ has operational soundness or adequacy;
  • either way, whether our construction is a distributive law.

Draft contribution paragraph (not yet reviewed by Eric):

Some configuration-based operational semantics already take a configuration to be an element of an effect monad applied to terms. Gavazzo and Faggian (LICS 2021) rewrite free-model elements for linear theories. Gavazzo, Treglia and Vanoni (ESOP 2024) and Dagnino, Giannini and Zucca (ECOOP 2025) advance every term of a monadic configuration at once, by Kleisli extension. Abou-Saleh and Pattinson (FoSSaCS 2013) give first-order While languages transition systems valued in the free model, and prove adequacy via final coalgebras. We bring this idea to a simply typed call-by-push-value calculus parameterised by a finitary algebraic theory whose free model monad is a monadic container. Within that setting we combine three things that none of the works we checked combines. (i) An Effect rule fires one operation at one position of the configuration, chosen by a one-hole context of the container, and flattens with the monad multiplication. (ii) Every step provably preserves the standard denotational semantics, which is defined independently of reduction. (iii) Reduction is strongly normalising whenever no effect step duplicates a subterm, and at ground types it ends in the denotation. The same generic theorems cover global state, which Gavazzo and Faggian's rewriting theory excludes, and errors and weighted lists, which have no comodel in Set. Several ingredients have precedents: soundness and termination for effect trees (Plotkin and Power, FoSSaCS 2001), step-wise invariance for single effects, and monadic containers. The contribution is their effect-generic combination and the position-focused formulation of the effect step.

Our weaknesses relative to these works (be honest in the related-work section):

  • no recursion, where Plotkin–Power 2001, JSV, Kavvos and DGZ have it;
  • no contextual equivalence;
  • polynomial free models only, so no multisets or multidistributions, which GF21 and Faggian–Ronchi Della Rocca cover.

5. Optional, but recommended or invited

  • Adequacy corollary. This is cheap, and it answers Reviewer A's framing of the paper as "soundness and adequacy". For an affine model and closed $M : FA$ with $A$ ground, $\eta(M) \mapsto^* c$ with $c \cong \llbracket M\rrbracket$. It follows from three things:

    • SN plus a unique final configuration;
    • step soundness;
    • ground closed values being in bijection with $\llbracket A\rrbracket$.

    Corollary: $\llbracket M\rrbracket = \llbracket N\rrbracket$ implies that $M$ and $N$ reach the same final configuration in every ground closing context. Cite Plotkin–Power 2001 for this form. Do not claim full abstraction, recursion, or anything beyond affine models. This depends on the §2 repairs going through.

  • Non-affine examples from the rebuttal. Reviewer A says they are welcome but not required.

    • Duplication combinator: operations $d$ (arity 0) and $\cdot$ (arity 2), with $d\cdot x = x\cdot x = x\cdot d$. This is a monadic container that is not weakly normalizing: $\langle x\cdot y, (d/x, d/y)\rangle$ steps back to itself.
    • Unital near-semiring: $x*(y+z) = x*y + x*z$ duplicates $x$, yet the system is SN under a bespoke variant.

    Conclusion: affinity is common and easy to check, but it is not the most general condition.

  • Future work (Reviewer A, optional): discrete generalized polynomial functors, which may not be the right level of generality; infinite arities; right modules and local state. Combining theories need not stay polynomial. For example, monoids ⊗ monoids gives commutative monoids, whose free model, finite multisets, is not polynomial.

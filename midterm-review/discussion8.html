1. Termination

Definition (terminates). \(e\) terminates when \(e \longrightarrow^* v\) for some value \(v\).

Theorem (termination). If \(\vdash e : \tau\) then \(e\) terminates.

Example. With \(\mathtt{twice} = \lambda f{:}\mathsf{int} \to \mathsf{int}.\, \lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, f\,(f\,x)\) and \(\mathtt{succ} = \lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, x + 1\):

\[ \mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ}\ 2 \longrightarrow (\lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, \mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,x))\, 2 \longrightarrow \mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,2) \longrightarrow^* 4 \]

terminates, as stated.

2. Attempt: induction on the typing derivation

Attempt. Induction on \(\vdash e : \tau\). Cases \(n\), \(\mathsf{true}\), \(\mathsf{false}\), \(\lambda x{:}\tau.\, e\): already values. Case \(x\): no closed variable. Case \(e_1\, e_2\):

  • Inversion: \(\vdash e_1 : \tau_1 \to \tau_2\) and \(\vdash e_2 : \tau_1\).
  • Inductive hypotheses: \(e_1 \longrightarrow^* \lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e_1'\) (a value of function type is a λ, by preservation and canonical forms) and \(e_2 \longrightarrow^* v\) for some value \(v\).
  • Run: \(e_1\, e_2 \longrightarrow^* (\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e_1')\, v \longrightarrow e_1'[x \mapsto v]\).
  • Needed: \(e_1'[x \mapsto v]\) terminates. There is no hypothesis about \(e_1'[x \mapsto v]\). Stuck.

Example. On \(\mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ}\ 2\): the hypotheses are \(\mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ} \longrightarrow^* \lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, \mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,x)\) and \(2 \longrightarrow^* 2\). The run reaches \(\mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,2)\). Needed: \(\mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,2)\) terminates. Nothing in the hypotheses mentions \(\mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,2)\).

Remark. "\(e_1'[x \mapsto v]\) is well typed, so apply the inductive hypothesis" is invalid: the hypothesis is for subderivations, and the derivation of \(e_1'[x \mapsto v]\) is not one. \(v\) is copied into every \(x\), so that derivation can be larger than those of \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) together.

The attempt in Rocq, with preservation along a run assumed. The proof is aborted at the goal above.

3. The logical relation

Definition (good values \(\mathcal{V}_\tau\), good expressions \(\mathcal{E}_\tau\)). By recursion on \(\tau\). \(\mathcal{V}_\tau\) is a set of closed values.

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{int}} &= \{\, n \mid n \in \mathbb{Z} \,\} \\ \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{bool}} &= \{\, \mathsf{true}, \mathsf{false} \,\} \\ \mathcal{V}_{\tau_1 \to \tau_2} &= \{\, v \mid \forall v' \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_1}.\ v\, v' \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_2} \,\} \\ \mathcal{E}_\tau &= \{\, e \mid e \longrightarrow^* v \text{ and } v \in \mathcal{V}_\tau \,\} \end{aligned} \]

Example. \(\mathtt{succ} \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{int} \to \mathsf{int}}\): for \(n \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{int}}\), \(\mathtt{succ}\, n \longrightarrow n + 1 \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{int}}\). \(\mathtt{twice} \in \mathcal{V}_{(\mathsf{int} \to \mathsf{int}) \to \mathsf{int} \to \mathsf{int}}\): for \(g \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{int} \to \mathsf{int}}\), \(\mathtt{twice}\, g \longrightarrow \lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, g\,(g\,x)\), and for \(n \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathsf{int}}\), \((\lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, g\,(g\,x))\, n \longrightarrow g\,(g\,n)\), which reaches a number because \(g\) is good, applied twice.

Lemma. If \(v \in \mathcal{V}_\tau\) then \(v\) is a value and \(\vdash v : \tau\). (In Rocq both are part of the function clause.)

Lemma (anti-reduction). If \(e \longrightarrow e'\) and \(e' \in \mathcal{E}_\tau\) then \(e \in \mathcal{E}_\tau\). Likewise for \(e \longrightarrow^* e'\).

\(\mathcal{V}\) and \(\mathcal{E}\); the two facts; anti-reduction.

Attempt (closed statement). If \(\vdash e : \tau\) then \(e \in \mathcal{E}_\tau\). Induction on \(\vdash e : \tau\).

  • Case \(e_1\, e_2\): \(e_1 \longrightarrow^* \lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e_1' \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_1 \to \tau_2}\) and \(e_2 \longrightarrow^* v \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_1}\). By the function clause, \((\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e_1')\, v \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_2}\). By anti-reduction, \(e_1\, e_2 \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_2}\). Closes.
  • Case \(\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e'\): it is a value, so it must be in \(\mathcal{V}_{\tau_1 \to \tau_2}\). Take \(v \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_1}\); \((\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e')\, v \longrightarrow e'[x \mapsto v]\); needed \(e'[x \mapsto v] \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_2}\). \(e'\) is open, the statement is about closed terms: no hypothesis. Stuck.

The attempt in Rocq, aborted at the λ case.

4. The fundamental theorem

Definition (closing substitution). \(\gamma\) is a list of pairs \(x \mapsto v\); \(e[\gamma]\) applies them in order. The good substitutions for \(\Gamma\), and the semantic judgement:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_{\cdot} &= \{\, \cdot \,\} \\ \mathcal{G}_{\Gamma, x:\tau} &= \{\, (\gamma, x \mapsto v) \mid \gamma \in \mathcal{G}_\Gamma,\ v \in \mathcal{V}_\tau \,\} \\ \Gamma \models e \in \tau &:= \forall \gamma \in \mathcal{G}_\Gamma.\ e[\gamma] \in \mathcal{E}_\tau \end{aligned} \]

\(e[\gamma]\), \(\mathcal{G}_\Gamma\), and dropping a variable from \(\gamma\).

Theorem (fundamental). If \(\Gamma \vdash e : \tau\) then \(\Gamma \models e \in \tau\). Induction on \(\Gamma \vdash e : \tau\); let \(\gamma \in \mathcal{G}_\Gamma\).

  • Case \(x\) with \(x{:}\tau \in \Gamma\): \(x[\gamma]\) is the value \(\gamma\) gives \(x\), which is in \(\mathcal{V}_\tau\) by the definition of \(\mathcal{G}_\Gamma\), hence in \(\mathcal{E}_\tau\).
  • Case \(e_1\, e_2\): \((e_1\, e_2)[\gamma] = e_1[\gamma]\ e_2[\gamma]\). Inductive hypotheses: \(e_1[\gamma] \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_1 \to \tau_2}\), \(e_2[\gamma] \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_1}\). Then as in the closed attempt: \(e_1[\gamma] \longrightarrow^* \lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e_1'\), \(e_2[\gamma] \longrightarrow^* v\), \((\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e_1')\, v \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_2}\) by the function clause, and anti-reduction.
  • Case \(\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e'\) with \(\Gamma, x{:}\tau_1 \vdash e' : \tau_2\): \((\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e')[\gamma] = \lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e'[\gamma]\), with \(x\) not in \(\gamma\) by α-renaming. It is a value. For \(v \in \mathcal{V}_{\tau_1}\): \((\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e'[\gamma])\, v \longrightarrow e'[\gamma][x \mapsto v] = e'[\gamma, x \mapsto v]\), and \((\gamma, x \mapsto v) \in \mathcal{G}_{\Gamma, x:\tau_1}\), so the inductive hypothesis for \(e'\) gives \(e'[\gamma, x \mapsto v] \in \mathcal{E}_{\tau_2}\). Anti-reduction.

Corollary (termination). Take \(\Gamma = \cdot\), \(\gamma = \cdot\); \(e[\cdot] = e\), so \(e \in \mathcal{E}_\tau\), so \(e \longrightarrow^* v \in \mathcal{V}_\tau\), a value.

Remark. In Rocq the induction is on \(e\), inverting the typing rule at each step, so that at a λ the context can be changed from \(\Gamma, x{:}\tau_1\) to \(\Gamma\) with its old \(x\) dropped. The substitution facts used are folded below.

Substitution lemmas

Free variables; closed terms.

A closed well-typed term has no free variables.

Substituting for a non-free variable does nothing; a closed value kills the variable; substitutions for different variables commute.

A closed term is unchanged by \(\gamma\); a single substitution commutes with a \(\gamma\) not mentioning its variable.

\(e[\gamma]\) through each form of \(e\).

Good substitutions are closed, send each variable to a good value, and survive dropping a variable.

Lookup after drop and append; \(e[\gamma]\) is typed under the leftover context; the context swap at a λ.

The theorem and the corollary. Print Assumptions: closed under the global context.

5. Denotational semantics

Definition (types and contexts as sets).

\[ \begin{aligned} [\![\mathsf{int}]\!] &= \mathbb{Z} & [\![\mathsf{bool}]\!] &= \{\mathsf{true}, \mathsf{false}\} & [\![\tau_1 \to \tau_2]\!] &= [\![\tau_1]\!] \to [\![\tau_2]\!] \\ [\![\cdot]\!] &= \{*\} & [\![\Gamma, x{:}\tau]\!] &= [\![\Gamma]\!] \times [\![\tau]\!] \end{aligned} \]

Definition (terms as functions). For \(\Gamma \vdash e : \tau\), \([\![e]\!] : [\![\Gamma]\!] \to [\![\tau]\!]\), by recursion on the derivation:

\[ \begin{aligned} [\![x]\!]\,\gamma &= \gamma(x) \\ [\![n]\!]\,\gamma &= n \qquad [\![\mathsf{true}]\!]\,\gamma = \mathsf{true} \qquad [\![\mathsf{false}]\!]\,\gamma = \mathsf{false} \\ [\![e_1 + e_2]\!]\,\gamma &= [\![e_1]\!]\,\gamma + [\![e_2]\!]\,\gamma \\ [\![\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e]\!]\,\gamma &= z \mapsto [\![e]\!]\,(\gamma, x \mapsto z) \\ [\![e_1\, e_2]\!]\,\gamma &= ([\![e_1]\!]\,\gamma)\,([\![e_2]\!]\,\gamma) \\ [\![\mathsf{if}\ e\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2]\!]\,\gamma &= \begin{cases} [\![e_1]\!]\,\gamma & [\![e]\!]\,\gamma = \mathsf{true} \\ [\![e_2]\!]\,\gamma & [\![e]\!]\,\gamma = \mathsf{false} \end{cases} \end{aligned} \]

A closed term is a function from \(\{*\}\): an element \([\![e]\!](*) \in [\![\tau]\!]\).

Example. \([\![\mathtt{succ}]\!](*) = n \mapsto n + 1\). \([\![\mathtt{twice}]\!](*) = g \mapsto n \mapsto g(g(n))\). \([\![\mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ}\ 2]\!](*) = ([\![\mathtt{twice}]\!](*))\,([\![\mathtt{succ}]\!](*))\,(2) = 4\).

Example (open term). \(y{:}\mathsf{int}, x{:}\mathsf{int} \vdash \mathsf{if}\ \mathsf{true}\ \mathsf{then}\ x\ \mathsf{else}\ y\). An element of \([\![\Gamma]\!]\) is \(((*, 10), 20)\), innermost first: \(x = 10\), \(y = 20\). The meaning at it is \(10\).

\([\![\tau]\!]\) and \([\![\Gamma]\!]\) as Rocq types.

The derivation as data (deriv, in Type, variables by position), \(\gamma(x)\), and \([\![e]\!]\).

\(\mathtt{succ}\), \(\mathtt{twice}\), and \(\mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ}\ 2\), computed.

6. Soundness and adequacy

Theorem (soundness). If \(e \longrightarrow^* v\) then \([\![e]\!] = [\![v]\!]\). By induction on the run, from the one-step lemma.

Lemma (one step). If \(e \longrightarrow e'\) then \([\![e]\!] = [\![e']\!]\). Functions are equal when equal at every \(\gamma\); one case per rule.

  • \(\mathsf{if}\ \mathsf{true}\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2 \longrightarrow e_1\): \([\![\mathsf{if}\ \mathsf{true}\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2]\!]\,\gamma = [\![e_1]\!]\,\gamma\), by the \(\mathsf{if}\) clause, since \([\![\mathsf{true}]\!]\,\gamma = \mathsf{true}\).
  • \(n_1 + n_2 \longrightarrow n_3\): both sides are the integer \(n_1 + n_2\).
  • \((\lambda x{:}\tau.\, e)\, v \longrightarrow e[x \mapsto v]\): \([\![(\lambda x{:}\tau.\, e)\, v]\!]\,\gamma = [\![e]\!]\,(\gamma, x \mapsto [\![v]\!]\,\gamma) = [\![e[x \mapsto v]]\!]\,\gamma\), the last step by compositionality.
  • \(E[r] \longrightarrow E[r']\): \([\![E[r]]\!]\,\gamma = ([\![E]\!]\,\gamma)([\![r]\!]\,\gamma) = ([\![E]\!]\,\gamma)([\![r']\!]\,\gamma) = [\![E[r']]\!]\,\gamma\), by compositionality for contexts and the redex case.

Lemma (compositionality). \([\![e[x \mapsto v]]\!]\,\gamma = [\![e]\!]\,(\gamma, x \mapsto [\![v]\!]\,\gamma)\), by induction on \(e\). For a context, \([\![E[e]]\!]\,\gamma = ([\![E]\!]\,\gamma)([\![e]\!]\,\gamma)\): a context is a term with a variable for the hole.

Example. \(\mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ}\ 2 \longrightarrow (\lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, \mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,x))\, 2\), and \([\![\mathtt{twice}\ \mathtt{succ}\ 2]\!](*) = 4 = [\![(\lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, \mathtt{succ}\,(\mathtt{succ}\,x))\, 2]\!](*)\).

The \(\mathsf{if}\) and \(+\) cases, by reflexivity; the step on the example, and both meanings computed.

Theorem (adequacy). If \(\vdash e : \mathsf{bool}\) then \(e \longrightarrow^* [\![e]\!](*)\). Likewise at \(\mathsf{int}\), and at every type without \(\to\) in it. Not as stated at \(\tau_1 \to \tau_2\): \(e\) runs to a λ, not to a function.

Definition (binary logical relation). \(v \approx^{\mathcal{V}}_\tau x\) between a closed value and \(x \in [\![\tau]\!]\); \(e \approx^{\mathcal{E}}_\tau x\) between a closed expression and \(x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} n \approx^{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{int}} m &\iff n = m \\ b \approx^{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathsf{bool}} b' &\iff b = b' \\ v \approx^{\mathcal{V}}_{\tau_1 \to \tau_2} f &\iff \forall v' \approx^{\mathcal{V}}_{\tau_1} x.\ v\, v' \approx^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau_2} f(x) \\ e \approx^{\mathcal{E}}_\tau x &\iff e \longrightarrow^* v \text{ and } v \approx^{\mathcal{V}}_\tau x \end{aligned} \]

Theorem (fundamental, binary). If \(\Gamma \vdash e : \tau\) and \(\gamma\) relates each variable's value to its denotation, then \(e[\gamma] \approx^{\mathcal{E}}_\tau [\![e]\!]\,\gamma\). Proof as in section 4. Adequacy is the case \(\Gamma = \cdot\), \(\tau = \mathsf{bool}\).

Adequacy stated (proof not in this file) and the relation.

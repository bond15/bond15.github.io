The simply typed λ-calculus: type soundness

Only what type soundness needs, with evaluation given by evaluation contexts. Each piece of math is followed by its Rocq code from Discussion 6/STLC_Soundness.v, in file order.

1. Syntax

\[ \begin{aligned} \tau &::= \mathsf{int} \mid \mathsf{bool} \mid \tau_1 \to \tau_2 \\ e &::= n \mid \mathsf{true} \mid \mathsf{false} \mid x \mid \lambda x{:}\tau.\, e \mid e_1\, e_2 \\ &\quad\mid e_1 + e_2 \mid \mathsf{if}\ e\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2 \\ v &::= n \mid \mathsf{true} \mid \mathsf{false} \mid \lambda x{:}\tau.\, e \end{aligned} \]

Here \(n\) ranges over integers and \(x\) over variable names. A value \(v\) is a finished answer.

In Rocq, the language's symbols differ from Rocq's own, so they can't be confused: ⊕ is \(+\), TRUE/FALSE are \(\mathsf{true}\)/\(\mathsf{false}\), INT/BOOL/⇒ are the types, and e1 · e2 is application.

Substitution

\(e[x \mapsto v]\) replaces the free \(x\)'s in \(e\) by \(v\) (subst):

\[ y[x \mapsto v] = \begin{cases} v & y = x \\ y & y \ne x \end{cases} \]
\[ (\lambda y{:}\tau.\, e)[x \mapsto v] = \begin{cases} \lambda y{:}\tau.\, e & y = x \\ \lambda y{:}\tau.\, e[x \mapsto v] & y \ne x \end{cases} \]

When \(y = x\), the inner \(\lambda\) owns \(x\), so we stop. On every other form, substitution goes into the subterms.

Why it isn't capture-avoiding. Capture happens when \(v\) has a free variable \(y\) and substitution carries it under a \(\lambda y\) inside \(e\), where it gets bound by mistake. Avoiding that means renaming \(\lambda y\) first. Here it can't happen, because every \(v\) we substitute is closed: it has no free variables at all.

The reason is that evaluation contexts have no \(\lambda x{:}\tau.\, E\) form, so evaluation never looks inside a \(\lambda\). In a closed program, the redex \(r\) in \(E[r]\) is not under any binder, so it is closed too, and so is the argument \(v\) of a β-redex \((\lambda x{:}\tau.\, e)\, v\). In the proof this shows up in two places: the substitution lemma assumes \(\vdash v : \tau_1\) in the empty context, and the replacement lemma (§4) types the redex in the empty context.

Where it would go wrong: if we also reduced under \(\lambda\), then in \(\lambda y{:}\mathsf{int}.\, (\lambda x{:}\mathsf{int}.\, \lambda y{:}\mathsf{int}.\, x)\, y\) the inner β-step would give \(\lambda y{:}\mathsf{int}.\, y\) instead of a function that returns the outer \(y\).

2. Evaluation contexts and \(\longrightarrow\)

An evaluation context is a term with one hole \([\,]\), at the place where the next step happens:

\[ \begin{aligned} E ::= {} & [\,] \mid E\, e \mid v\, E \mid E + e \mid n + E \\ \mid {} & \mathsf{if}\ E\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2 \end{aligned} \]

Read it as "where to look next": the function, then the argument; the left summand, then the right one; the condition of an if. There is no \(\lambda x{:}\tau.\, E\).

In \(v\, E\), \(v\) ranges over values, a subset of expressions. In Rocq that subset is the type val \(= \{ e \mid e \text{ is a value} \}\): an expression together with a proof that it is a value. exp_of v is the expression part.

\(E[e]\) is \(E\) with \(e\) put in the hole (plug). For example, \((1 + 2) + 3 = E[1 + 2]\) with \(E = [\,] + 3\).

Head steps \(r \longrightarrow_h r'\) (head): the four rules that do work, at the top of the term. The \(r\) on the left is called a redex.

\[(\lambda x{:}\tau.\, e)\, v \longrightarrow_h e[x \mapsto v]\]
\[n_1 + n_2 \longrightarrow_h n_3 \quad (n_3 = n_1 + n_2)\]
\[\mathsf{if}\ \mathsf{true}\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2 \longrightarrow_h e_1\]
\[\mathsf{if}\ \mathsf{false}\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2 \longrightarrow_h e_2\]

The step relation (step) is one rule: take the redex out of its context, do the work, put the result back.

\[\frac{r \longrightarrow_h r'}{E[r] \longrightarrow E[r']}\]

With \(E = [\,]\) this says that a head step is a step (head_step).

\(e \longrightarrow^* e'\) is zero or more steps.

A term is stuck if it is not a value and cannot step. Example: \(\mathsf{true} + 1\). To check it, go through the ways to write it as \(E[r]\): with \(E = [\,]\), no head rule adds a Boolean; with \(E = E' + 1\), we'd need \(E'[r] = \mathsf{true}\), which no redex gives.

3. Typing

A context lists variables with their types:

\[\Gamma ::= \cdot \mid \Gamma, x{:}\tau\]

\(x{:}\tau \in \Gamma\) means the rightmost \(x\) in \(\Gamma\) has type \(\tau\). So later bindings shadow earlier ones, just as an inner \(\lambda x\) shadows an outer one. \(\vdash e : \tau\) means \(\cdot \vdash e : \tau\).

\[\frac{}{\Gamma \vdash n : \mathsf{int}}\]
\[\frac{}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{true} : \mathsf{bool}}\]
\[\frac{}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{false} : \mathsf{bool}}\]
\[\frac{x{:}\tau \in \Gamma}{\Gamma \vdash x : \tau}\]
\[\frac{\Gamma, x{:}\tau_1 \vdash e : \tau_2}{\Gamma \vdash \lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e : \tau_1 \to \tau_2}\]
\[\frac{\Gamma \vdash e_1 : \tau_1 \to \tau_2 \quad \Gamma \vdash e_2 : \tau_1}{\Gamma \vdash e_1\, e_2 : \tau_2}\]
\[\frac{\Gamma \vdash e_1 : \mathsf{int} \quad \Gamma \vdash e_2 : \mathsf{int}}{\Gamma \vdash e_1 + e_2 : \mathsf{int}}\]
\[\frac{\Gamma \vdash e : \mathsf{bool} \quad \Gamma \vdash e_1 : \tau \quad \Gamma \vdash e_2 : \tau}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{if}\ e\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2 : \tau}\]

The stuck term \(\mathsf{true} + 1\) has no type: the \(+\) rule needs \(\vdash \mathsf{true} : \mathsf{int}\), and the only rule for \(\mathsf{true}\) gives \(\mathsf{bool}\).

4. Type soundness

Canonical forms. For a value \(v\):

  • if \(\vdash v : \mathsf{int}\) then \(v = n\);
  • if \(\vdash v : \mathsf{bool}\) then \(v\) is \(\mathsf{true}\) or \(\mathsf{false}\);
  • if \(\vdash v : \tau_1 \to \tau_2\) then \(v = \lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e\).

Proof. Go through the four kinds of value. For the wrong kinds, no typing rule gives that type.

Progress. If \(\vdash e : \tau\), then \(e\) is a value or \(e \longrightarrow e'\) for some \(e'\).

Proof. By induction on the typing derivation. A variable can't be typed in \(\cdot\). For \(e_1\, e_2\), \(e_1 + e_2\) and \(\mathsf{if}\), look at the subterm in evaluation position:

  • If it steps, it is \(E[r] \longrightarrow E[r']\). Add one layer of context: for example \(e_1 = E[r]\) gives \(e_1\, e_2 = (E\, e_2)[r]\).
  • If it is a value, canonical forms says what it looks like, and the whole term is a redex. Use \(E = [\,]\).

Weakening. Write \(\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'\) for: every \(x{:}\tau \in \Gamma\) also has \(x{:}\tau \in \Gamma'\). If \(\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma'\) and \(\Gamma \vdash e : \tau\), then \(\Gamma' \vdash e : \tau\).

By induction on the derivation; only the variable and \(\lambda\) rules look at \(\Gamma\).

Substitution lemma. If \(\Gamma, x{:}\tau_1 \vdash e : \tau\) and \(\vdash v : \tau_1\), then \(\Gamma \vdash e[x \mapsto v] : \tau\).

Proof. By induction on \(e\). The interesting cases:

  • \(e = y\), \(y = x\). Then \(\tau = \tau_1\) and \(e[x \mapsto v] = v\). Weaken \(\vdash v : \tau_1\) from \(\cdot\) to \(\Gamma\).
  • \(e = y\), \(y \ne x\). The variable stays, and \(y{:}\tau \in \Gamma\).
  • \(e = \lambda y{:}\tau_y.\, e'\), \(y = x\). Substitution stops. The inner \(x\) hides the outer one, so \(\Gamma, x{:}\tau_1, x{:}\tau_y \subseteq \Gamma, x{:}\tau_y\), and weakening moves the body's typing over.
  • \(e = \lambda y{:}\tau_y.\, e'\), \(y \ne x\). Swap to \(\Gamma, y{:}\tau_y, x{:}\tau_1\) by weakening, then use the IH.

Head preservation. If \(\vdash r : \tau\) and \(r \longrightarrow_h r'\), then \(\vdash r' : \tau\).

Proof. The \(+\) and \(\mathsf{if}\) rules are immediate. For β: from \(\vdash (\lambda x{:}\tau_1.\, e)\, v : \tau\) we get \(x{:}\tau_1 \vdash e : \tau\) and \(\vdash v : \tau_1\), so \(\vdash e[x \mapsto v] : \tau\) by the substitution lemma.

Replacement. If \(\vdash E[r] : \tau\), then there is a \(\tau'\) with \(\vdash r : \tau'\), and \(\vdash E[r'] : \tau\) for every \(r'\) with \(\vdash r' : \tau'\).

So \(E\) only cares about the type of what's in its hole. The redex is typed in the empty context because \(E\) binds no variables. By induction on \(E\): invert the typing rule for the outer layer and use the IH on the inner one.

Preservation. If \(\vdash e : \tau\) and \(e \longrightarrow e'\), then \(\vdash e' : \tau\).

Proof. The step is \(E[r] \longrightarrow E[r']\) with \(r \longrightarrow_h r'\). Replacement gives \(\vdash r : \tau'\); head preservation gives \(\vdash r' : \tau'\); replacement again gives \(\vdash E[r'] : \tau\).

Type soundness. If \(\vdash e : \tau\) and \(e \longrightarrow^* e'\), then \(e'\) is not stuck: it is a value or it steps.

Proof. By induction on the number of steps. Preservation keeps \(\vdash e' : \tau\) along the way, and progress applies at the end.
